Responding to a Delayed Loan Repayment Proposal

This article summarises the position set out in my letter "Tanggapan atas Surat Tanggal 6 November 2026" (written in Indonesian). Read the full letter (PDF).

Background

In March 2023 I entered into a loan agreement with PT Multi Makmur ("PTMM"). The principal was Rp2,000,000,000, and the agreement also provided for a commission on certain trade transactions. Under Article 6, the loan was due for repayment by 1 June 2023. More than three years later, final settlement has not been reached.

On 6 November 2026 I received a letter proposing that repayment begin on 25 March 2027, after a further transaction (referred to as "CMG") was planned for February 2027. This article explains how I responded and why.

Repayment of the loan stands apart from commissions

The central point of my reply is that two things in the agreement are separate:

  • Repayment of the principal is an obligation under the agreement and does not depend on whether any business transaction goes ahead.
  • Commission is an additional benefit, payable only if the conditions in Article 7 are met (USD 1.30 per metric ton, supported by transaction documents such as the Bill of Lading).

For that reason I declined to take part in the CMG transaction and asked that repayment not be tied to it, to shipments, or to other business arrangements. Declining does not waive my right to review and receive any commission that has already arisen under Article 7.

Why I could not accept a 2027 start

The proposal did not state payment amounts, instalment stages or a full settlement schedule, and it would have pushed repayment of an overdue loan into 2027. I asked instead for a clear and workable payment plan: the date of each payment, the amount, and the method.

Working out the final figure

Once a payment plan is agreed, both sides can reconcile the final amount, taking into account:

  1. The original principal of Rp2,000,000,000 (Article 5), less payments received, to find the outstanding balance.
  2. Commission actually owed under Article 7.
  3. The annual late-payment penalty of 18% under Article 9, calculated pro rata over the actual period of delay.
  4. All payments already made, checked against transfer records.

I confirmed that I received the Rp300,000,000 payment of 25 August 2023 mentioned in the 6 November letter. How that amount should be applied is for both sides to decide after reviewing the agreement and the payment records, and the figures should then be confirmed in writing.

Proposed timeline

  • 20 November 2026: a written payment plan from PTMM.
  • 1 December 2026: payment of the outstanding principal, plus the agreed commission and penalty.

I also hold three cheques provided as security under Article 8. I suggested they be returned only after payment is completed and both sides have confirmed that all obligations under the agreement are settled.

An official reply from the company

The 6 November letter was sent in a personal capacity, not as a formal letter from PTMM. Because the agreement is between me and the company, I asked for the reply to be issued on PTMM letterhead and signed by its Director and Commissioner. It should confirm the company's position and set out the payment dates, amounts, method and stages.

Under Article 20, any change to the agreement must be made in a written addendum. Discussing a payment plan does not mean I have agreed to change the repayment deadline or given up any existing right.

Closing

I remain hopeful that this can be settled in a reasonable and respectful way. After more than three years, what both sides need is a concrete result, not repeated discussion without certainty.
